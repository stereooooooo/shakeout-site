How this works

Shakeout adds the forecast air temperature and dew point (°F) for the hour you run and slows your paces using its own interpolated version of a widely used coaching heuristic (Hadley, 2013): no change below a combined 110, rising to about 10% at 180, beyond which run by effort. It hasn't been validated in studies.

For background on how heat affects running, see Ely et al. (2007) and Mantzios et al. (2022).

It's general guidance, not medical advice. Your response will vary: slower or unacclimatised runners may slow more, and sun and wind change things too. Check with your doctor about exercising in heat.

For short repeats, some coaches halve the adjustment.

The table

Always in °F, even when the app shows °C: the model is defined on °F sums.

Between rows, the adjustment is a straight line. Every pace slows by the same %, rounded to the nearest 5 seconds, and never rounds faster than usual.

For example: air 72°F, dew point 68°F. The sum is 140, so paces are 3% slower, and an easy 7:42 per mile becomes 7:55.

The words

Each hour gets one word for its air, each with its own colour.

Ideal
Sum under 110. No adjustment.
Fair
Sum 110–130. About 1–2% slower.
Warm
Sum 131–145 in drier air (dew point under 60). About 2–4% slower.
Humid
Sum 131–145 with dew point 60 or more. About 2–4% slower.
Muggy
Sum 146–160. About 4–6% slower.
Brutal
Sum 161–180. About 6–10% slower.
Extreme
Over 180. No pace targets: run by effort.
Rain
Rain likely this hour: 50% or more.
Snow
Snow, sleet or freezing rain likely this hour.
Windy
Sustained 15 mph, or gusts 25.
Crisp

Cold

Biting
Feels like below 20°F.

Disclaimer

Shakeout's pace adjustments are estimates from a temperature-plus-dew-point heuristic, for healthy, heat-acclimatised runners. They aren't medical advice. Check with your doctor about exercising in heat, and stop if you feel unwell.
